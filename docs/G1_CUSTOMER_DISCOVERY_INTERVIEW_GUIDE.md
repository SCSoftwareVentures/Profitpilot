# G1 Customer Discovery Interview Guide

**Status:** research instrument only. It does not establish demand or authorise collection of customer records.
**Target hypothesis:** Australian independent commercial cleaning contractors with recurring, multi-site B2B contracts.
**Interview target:** 20 interviews, at least three recruitment channels, founder-network share no greater than 40%.
**Decision threshold:** at least 12/20 independently describe the same material pain before ProfitPilot is explained; at least eight quantified recent examples.

## 1. Research conduct

- Recruit people who perform, own, approve or pay for the relevant workflow. Record role and buying authority separately.
- Recruit through at least three independent channels. Track source so founder-network concentration can be calculated.
- Do not count multiple people from one business as independent businesses when assessing customer-level recurrence.
- Do not pitch ProfitPilot before the unaided problem section is complete.
- Ask for recent behaviour and artefacts only when necessary. Do not ask for passwords, broad exports, employee records or confidential contracts.
- Obtain permission before recording. Explain purpose, use, access, retention and deletion of notes/recordings. If the participant does not consent to recording, take minimal notes.
- Do not promise confidentiality beyond the actual storage and access controls. Do not place identifiable interview notes or contact details in this public repository.
- Keep verbatim participant statements distinct from interviewer interpretation.
- Do not treat politeness, feature enthusiasm, hypothetical interest or a request for a free trial as purchase intent.

## 3. Unaided problem discovery — do this before explaining the concept

1. "Walk me through the last time you worked out whether a recurring customer contract or site was performing as expected."
2. "What triggered that work?"
3. "Which systems, reports or people did you need?"
4. "Where did you have to re-enter, reconcile or estimate information?"
5. "What was the most difficult or time-consuming part?"
6. "What happened the last time the numbers or records did not line up?"
7. "How did you resolve it, and who made the final decision?"
8. "What was the consequence? Can you describe a specific recent example?"
9. "How often has that happened in the last three months?"
10. "What do you currently do to prevent it?"
11. "What have you already tried, including features or configuration in your existing systems?"
12. "Why did that work or not work?"

Use neutral probes: "What happened next?", "How do you know?", "Could you show me the steps without sharing confidential records?", "What did that cost in time or money?"

Do not introduce the words "profit leak", "automation", "AI", "reconciliation engine" or proposed feature names until this section is complete.

## 4. Quantify a recent example

For each concrete incident, record:

- approximate date and service period;
- contract/site count involved;
- trigger and workflow steps;
- people and roles involved;
- active customer/operator minutes;
- elapsed waiting time, separately;
- rework minutes;
- systems and exports used;
- approximate amount or range at issue, with currency and GST basis if known;
- how the amount was calculated and whether it was verified;
- decision delayed, changed or missed;
- operational/commercial consequence;
- current workaround and its cost;
- source of the estimate and confidence.

Never turn an estimate into a verified financial loss. If the participant cannot quantify an amount, record the limitation rather than forcing a number.

## 5. Current-stack and alternative assessment

- Which system is authoritative for contract scope and pricing?
- Which system is authoritative for payroll/timesheets?
- Where are supplier costs and customer invoices recorded?
- How are sites, contracts and service periods identified across systems?
- Which steps require spreadsheets or manual intervention?
- What does the existing software already do well?
- Which configuration, add-on, report or integration has been tried?
- What prevents the business from solving this with its current tools?
- What is the total cost of the current workflow, including staff time, subscriptions, add-ons and external support?
- What security, privacy, procurement or change-management requirements would a new supplier face?

Do not label a problem a market gap if ordinary configuration or an existing product resolves it adequately.

## 6. Buying process and willingness-to-pay evidence

Ask only after the problem and current workflow have been explored:

1. "Who would own a decision to change this process?"
2. "Who would approve a purchase and whose budget would it come from?"
3. "What would a solution need to demonstrate before you would consider paying?"
4. "What would block adoption?"
5. "What is the cost of leaving the process as it is?"
6. "Have you paid for a service or tool to address this before? What did you buy and what happened?"
7. "If a scoped service reliably produced the agreed outcome, what purchasing process would you follow?"

Record evidence in levels:
- **Weak:** positive reaction, compliments, hypothetical interest.
- **Moderate:** identifies budget owner, procurement path, measurable success condition and credible price range.
- **Strong:** requests a proposal, introduces the buyer, agrees a paid trial scope, or signs a paid commitment.
- **Commercial commitment:** payment or signed paid engagement actually secured and evidenced outside this public repository.

Do not record an interview as willingness-to-pay proof solely because the participant says "that sounds useful".

## 7. Concept disclosure — optional, only after unaided discovery

Use a neutral description:

"ProfitPilot is exploring a service that brings together authorised contract, labour, supplier-cost and invoice information for a recurring site/service period. It aims to show a traceable variance view, distinguish direct costs from approved allocations and unallocated amounts, and surface exceptions for human review. It is not an accounting or payroll replacement."

Then ask:
- "Which part, if any, addresses a problem you have already described?"
- "Which part is unnecessary or already handled?"
- "What would make the output untrustworthy?"
- "What evidence would you require before acting on it?"
- "What would you not allow a supplier to access?"
- "What would need to be true before you paid for it?"

Do not present roadmap features as implemented capabilities.

## 8. Coding and synthesis

Code each interview against:
- unaided pain described: yes/no;
- pain category, using participant language;
- recent concrete example: yes/no;
- quantified example: yes/no and method;
- frequency;
- material consequence;
- current workaround;
- existing-tool adequacy;
- independent recurrence;
- participant buying role;
- buyer identified;
- willingness-to-pay evidence level;
- key disconfirming evidence;
- follow-up evidence required.

Count the same pain as recurring only when the underlying problem and workflow are materially similar, not merely because several participants use the same broad word such as "admin".

## 9. G1 gate calculation

Report numerator and denominator for every threshold:
- interviews completed / 20;
- independent recruitment channels / 3;
- founder-network interviews / total interviews;
- unaided same-pain interviews / 20;
- quantified recent examples / 8;
- distinct businesses represented;
- buyer roles represented;
- exclusions and reasons.

G1 passes only when every stated threshold is met. Missing, ambiguous or post-pitch answers do not count as unaided pain evidence. A pass at G1 does not imply G0, G2, G3, G4 or G5 has passed.

## 10. Disconfirming evidence and stop conditions

Record evidence that weakens or invalidates the hypothesis, including:
- the issue is rare or low consequence;
- current systems already solve it adequately;
- data cannot lawfully or practically be accessed;
- the customer will not change its workflow;
- the buyer does not control budget;
- the cost to deliver exceeds plausible willingness to pay;
- the proposed output does not change a real decision;
- the pain belongs to a different customer segment or workflow.

Do not reinterpret negative evidence as a feature request. Recommend stop, pivot or further testing with the reason and evidence.
