# ProfitPilot — Commercial Model and Unit-Economics Validation Framework

**Status:** pricing hypotheses and validation method; no market pricing research or willingness-to-pay evidence is claimed.  
**Initial customer hypothesis:** Australian independent commercial cleaning contractors with recurring multi-site B2B contracts.  
**Commercial principle:** price for a repeatable business outcome and supportable service level, not for speculative “profit recovered”.

## 1. Commercial proposition to validate

ProfitPilot's proposed first value proposition is: reduce the time and uncertainty involved in reconciling contract/site/service-period revenue and delivery costs, while exposing material exceptions with traceable evidence.

This is a hypothesis. It must be supported by recent customer examples, measured time, repeated source feasibility, decisions informed and actual paid commitments.

Avoid unsupported claims such as:
- “guaranteed profit recovery”;
- “automatically finds all missed revenue”;
- “audited profit” or “compliant payroll”;
- “safeguarded funds” or “escrow” absent the exact legal/provider structure.

## 2. Pricing structures to test

Do not choose a final price from intuition or a competitor headline. Test clearly defined alternatives with target buyers after quantifying the problem.

| Model | Structure | Potential advantage | Risk to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat business subscription | One recurring fee for defined business-wide capability and limits | Simple to understand and budget | Larger multi-site businesses may consume disproportionate support/storage |
| Base + per-site tier | Base platform fee includes a site allowance; additional sites move to transparent tiers | Aligns price with operational footprint | Site count may not reflect data volume or value |
| Tiered subscription | Packages by sites, users and reporting/workflow depth | Supports expansion | Feature gates can obscure the core value |
| Setup/onboarding fee + subscription | One-off structured setup plus recurring software fee | Recognises migration and mapping effort | High setup fee may suppress adoption or expose poor repeatability |
| Concierge service fee | Fixed-scope paid reconciliation engagement | Tests willingness to pay before SaaS | Labour-heavy delivery may not scale |

Initial experiment: compare flat business fee, base-plus-site tier and fixed-scope paid concierge. Keep payment processing outside the first pricing proposition.

## 3. Willingness-to-pay experiment

For each qualified buyer:
1. Confirm the buyer controls or influences budget.
2. Review a quantified recent problem and current workaround.
3. Present a defined outcome, included workflows, limits, support and exclusions.
4. Offer a transparent price and billing cadence.
5. Ask what budget is currently spent on the problem or workaround.
6. Ask what would need to be true to justify purchase.
7. Record objection, alternative, timing, approval process and authority.
8. Request a concrete paid commitment or next procurement step.

Test price points through separate, comparable conversations rather than negotiating every buyer to a different scope. Retain exact offer, date, buyer type, price, terms and outcome. A survey answer or “sounds reasonable” is not a purchase.

## 4. Value measurement

Potential customer value categories:
- time saved in collecting, matching and reviewing records;
- fewer repeated reconciliation steps;
- faster identification of unexplained differences;
- improved traceability for management decisions;
- reduced delay in resolving valid variations or billing exceptions.

Value categories must not be double counted. A discrepancy identified is not automatically recoverable revenue. An approved variation is not collected cash. A management contribution figure is not statutory profit.

A conservative customer-value model should use measured hours and agreed loaded labour rates where the customer supplies them. Any financial recovery claim must have an independently verified baseline, customer decision, invoice/credit record and realised outcome evidence.

## 5. Unit economics model

Track these measures for each paid pilot and subscription cohort:

| Metric | Formula / definition |
|---|---|
| Monthly recurring revenue | Sum of active subscription recurring fees, excluding one-off setup and pass-through amounts |
| Annual recurring revenue | MRR × 12, only where recurring contracts and cancellations are treated consistently |
| Gross profit | Recognised revenue less direct cost to deliver service |
| Gross margin % | Gross profit / recognised revenue |
| Cost to serve | Allocated onboarding, support, processing, storage, infrastructure and third-party cost |
| Customer acquisition cost | Sales/marketing acquisition spend / new paying customers acquired in the same cohort definition |
| CAC payback months | CAC / monthly gross profit per acquired customer |
| Logo retention | Customers retained at period end / customers eligible at period start, adjusted for stated cohort rules |
| Expansion | Additional recurring revenue from existing customers, separated from new logo revenue |
| Support load | Support and analyst minutes per customer per period |
| Implementation effort | Initial configuration, data mapping, training and migration hours |

Do not invent threshold values before actual cost and retention evidence exists. Set target gross margin, CAC payback and support burden after paid concierge and pilot data.

## 6. Cost model inputs

Capture:
- hosting, database, object storage and backup;
- identity provider, email, messaging and monitoring;
- malware scanning and document processing;
- third-party integration/API charges;
- payment-provider charges only if a separately approved payment domain exists;
- customer onboarding and mapping labour;
- support and incident response;
- compliance, insurance, legal and security assurance;
- sales, acquisition and customer success;
- taxes and accounting treatment reviewed by qualified advisers.

Separate fixed, variable, per-tenant and per-transaction costs. Avoid treating founder labour as free when assessing long-term viability.

## 7. Packaging principles

- First package solves one recurring, measurable problem exceptionally well.
- Include source reconciliation, human review, provenance and reporting as core trust features, not premium add-ons that weaken safety.
- Price site/user/storage tiers transparently if actual cost-to-serve supports them.
- Keep implementation services and recurring software fees separately visible.
- Do not charge a percentage of “recovered profit” where attribution, approval or collection is disputed.
- Define support response and data export/deletion in each package.
- Keep future payments or marketplace services outside the MVP commercial offer until approved.

## 8. Commercial decision gates

Do not set a production price until:
- at least three paid concierge engagements/commitments exist under G3;
- service delivery effort and support burden are measured;
- at least three paid software pilots complete the G6 exit test;
- pricing and packaging are tested with the actual budget holder;
- gross margin and acquisition economics are calculated from real operating costs;
- customer terms and data-handling responsibilities receive legal review.

The purpose of this framework is to create a defensible pricing decision. It does not claim customer demand, pricing acceptance or positive unit economics.
