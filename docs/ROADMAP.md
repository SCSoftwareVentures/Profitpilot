# Roadmap and Scope Boundaries

## Stage 0 — Evidence before software
- Recruit target businesses and obtain authorised export sets.
- Test data feasibility and competitive alternatives.
- Run structured problem interviews.
- Deliver concierge one-site contract economics bridges.
- Measure customer effort, analyst effort, evidence coverage and decision usefulness.
- Secure paid concierge commitments before seeking software-build authorisation.

## Stage 1 — Minimum validated software wedge
Only after G5 signed GO:
- tenant and user access controls;
- contract, site and service-period records;
- authorised source import and immutable raw snapshots;
- reviewable mappings and import states;
- labour allocation with visible residuals;
- invoice matching suggestions with human confirmation;
- variation candidates and approval evidence;
- deterministic variance bridge;
- audit trail, export and exception reporting.

Exact release scope must be determined by observed concierge repetition and approved build capacity.

## Stage 2 — Controlled extensions
Potential later capabilities, subject to separate evidence and approval: multi-site expansion; subcontractor economics; customer quote and scope acceptance; change requests and variation approvals; transaction milestones and completion evidence; rectification and proportionate dispute workflow; payment-provider orchestration and payout reconciliation; aftercare, warranties and repeat-customer records; business profile and transaction-grounded reputation.

## Explicit non-goals for the initial wedge
Do not begin by building a full cleaning workforce-management suite, scheduling/rostering/attendance/GPS tracking, payroll or award interpretation, statutory tax calculations, accounting replacement or payment processing, job dispatch or inventory management, a full public marketplace, autonomous AI agents that approve consequential actions, unsupported visit-quality scores or worker performance rankings, or native mobile applications before a validated need.

## Release discipline
Every proposed feature must identify its user and problem, evidence supporting demand, source data and permission basis, decision/workflow changed, failure modes and human override, privacy/security/legal implications, implementation and support cost, and measurable acceptance criteria.

Feature count is not product strength. Do not expand scope merely because an idea is attractive.
